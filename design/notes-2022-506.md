# Notes on ePrint 2022/506

Groth and Shoup, *Design and analysis of a distributed ECDSA signing service*, ePrint 2022/506, version of 16 February 2023, 99 pages, `research/sources/eprint/2022-506.pdf`. Read in full from the PDF text: pp. 1 to 66 and appendix A, pp. 74 to 99. Reference list (pp. 66 to 74) not read. Page numbers are the printed numbers, which equal the PDF page numbers. SCHEME.md labels this paper [GS22b]; PROOFS.md labels it [GS22]. Its companion ePrint 2021/1330 is SCHEME's [GS22] and the paper's [GS21].

## Summary

The paper builds a threshold ECDSA service for the Internet Computer: `n` parties, `f < n/3` Byzantine, static corruption, asynchronous network, guaranteed output delivery, and one round of signature shares online (pp. 2, 11). Signing uses precomputed quadruples `[κ], [λ], [κλ], [αλ]` and a public presignature `R = g^κ`, re-randomized by `δ` from a random beacon that is drawn after the request is fixed, plus BIP32 additive key derivation (pp. 12 to 14, 92 to 93). Theorem 1 (p. 17) shows that the protocol realizes `F_ecdsa` in a hybrid world with `F_mpc`, a beacon and a random oracle for `H_delta`. `F_ecdsa` matches the attack game of 2021/1330 section 8.4, so unforgeability comes from Theorem 6 of 2021/1330 in the generic group model (p. 18).

`F_mpc` (Random, Open, OpenPower, Mul, Reshare) is realized by an asynchronous VSS on Pedersen commitments. A dealer encrypts all shares in one multi-recipient ciphertext, collects `2f + 1` signed verification shares into a certificate, and ACS agrees on a package of certified dealings (sections 3, 5, 7, 8). An honest party with a bad share posts a complaint that carries a decryption proof, and the others then open their shares (`FixBadShares`, pp. 42 to 43). Section 3.6 (p. 24) shows that key generation with Feldman instead of Pedersen dealings lets the adversary forge. Theorem 4 (p. 55) proves the realization of `F_mpc`, as a sketch of six games (pp. 55 to 58).

Section 4 defines the multi-encryption gadget (MEGa) with associated data, verifiable decryption, rogue keys allowed and no proof of possession (pp. 24 to 27). It gives the construction `E_dh` with the ephemeral-key proof `v' = H_G(enckey-pop, ad, v)^β` (p. 28) and proves ADO-CCA security under ICDH with three random oracles (Theorem 2, pp. 29 to 31). Section 4.3 drops the ephemeral-key proof, at the price of I2CDH and a rule that a decryption proof follows only another honest party's successful decryption (Theorem 3, pp. 32 to 34). Section 10 claims adaptive security of `E_dh` (Theorem 5, p. 62, with a loss of `n²`) and of the signing protocol under a stronger "linear discrete log" form of the ECDSA game (pp. 64 to 65), and sketches proactive security (p. 66). Appendix A gives the deployed encodings: hash to curve and hash to field from the IETF hash-to-curve draft 14 (later RFC 9380), the challenge inputs of both proofs (pp. 76 to 80), unmasked Feldman dealings derived from masked ones (pp. 82 to 88), BIP32 analysis (pp. 88 to 91), quadruple disposal and raw-hash signing (pp. 93 to 97), and random-oracle programming against SHA-256 (pp. 97 to 99).

The paper has no errata section. The p. 1 footnote says this version "contains significant optimizations with improved communication and computational complexity bounds not reported in some earlier versions (including the first 4 versions...)". The optimizations are described in sections 3.4.1 and 8.9. Earlier versions are not in the repository and were not compared. Every page PROOFS.md section 2 cites is correct for this file: p. 24 (no proof of possession), p. 27 (decryption queries only on `ad` "not previously submitted as part of an encryption query"), p. 28 (construction), p. 29 (ICDH, Theorem 2), pp. 30 to 31 (programming, game sequence), p. 32 (note 1: `ad` in `H_M` is not needed for `E_dh`).

## Does anything outside section 4 change or condition Theorem C?

Nothing outside section 4 breaks the way Theorem C uses the section 4 game sequence and the p. 27 rule. Five passages put conditions on it. Items 1, 2 and 5 report what the paper says. Items 3 and 4 are my analysis of Theorem C and SCHEME against the paper: item 3 is for the PROOFS.md reviewers, item 4 for SCHEME.

1. **The paper satisfies the p. 27 rule with signed dealer identities. Line dealing has no such mechanism, which is why Theorem C cannot use Theorem 2 as a black box.** The associated data is `(pkgID, dealerID)` (p. 38). "By including dealerID in the associated data, we effectively prevent a corrupt dealer from copying a dealing from an honest dealer" (p. 39, note 2). Game 1 of Theorem 4 "aborts the attack if any authenticity constraints are violated" (p. 55), and Game 4 is justified "because the associated data bound to dealings generated by honest dealers is disjoint from the associated data bound to dealings generated by corrupt dealers" (p. 56). The adaptive game also limits decryption queries to corrupt encryptors and grounds the limit in signatures: "messages sent by an honest party Pi will be authenticated by a secure signature" (p. 61, notes 1 and 2). A line-dealing message has no dealer signature that keeps its `ad` apart from an honest one. So the paper's way of meeting the rule is not available, and PROOFS.md §2.1 correctly gets disjointness instead from `SHA-256(ct)` in the `HG_eph` input plus the `Reuse` event.

2. **Line dealing must keep the ephemeral-key proof of section 4.1. It cannot drop it as section 4.3 does.** Nodes post COMPLAINT in Step A as soon as a share check fails. The paper delays complaints because broadcasting them early "would invalidate the security proof of our MEGa implementation if the simplified MEGa ... in Section 4.3 is used. However, if we use the MEGa E_dh in Section 4.1, it is safe to broadcasts complaints right away" (pp. 79 to 80). The deployed system uses 4.1 for the same reason and because 4.3 "does not provably provide adaptive security" (p. 75; p. 64, note 1). C3 and C4 meet this condition.

3. **(Analysis.) The paper's "soundness and ZK suffice" does not by itself cover the `Reuse` step of Theorem C. The bound stands; one sentence is missing from the proof.** Inside section 4 the paper says its sequence "shows that for the PoKs, we only need soundness and ZK, and do not need simulation soundness" (p. 31). Section 9 repeats the point: "because we do not need to invoke the soundness property again, we do not require simulation soundness" (p. 56). In Theorem 2, soundness is used only in Game 3, while honest proofs are still real. Theorem C does the same in its Game 3. But as written it also bounds `Reuse` "unless soundness fails (`Q_ephpok/q`)" in Game 6, which invokes soundness after Game 4 has replaced the honest dealers' proofs with simulated ones. Either of two arguments closes this. First, a `Reuse` message has an adversarial `HG_eph` input, so the Game 3 soundness bound already covers it, and the hops to Game 6 change its probability only through programming failures already counted in `δ_C`. Second, directly in the random oracle model: a `Reuse` message differs from every honest message in `(hd, SHA-256(ct))` (after the collision abort of Game 0), and that prefix starts every `Hq_ephpok` input. The adversary's challenge is therefore never a programmed point, and the per-query bound `1/q` is unchanged. The same applies to `LD1/rs-ephpok`. The paper's own instantiation hashes the same kind of prefix into its challenge, `γ ← hash_to_field(enckey-pop-challenge; pkgID, dealerID, v, v', v̂, v̂')` (p. 77). PROOFS.md should add one of these sentences. As it stands, its Game 3 remark ("plain soundness suffices, as [GS22] p. 31 remarks") reads as if it also covered `Reuse`.

4. **(Analysis.) Group membership applies to every point in the reduction, registered keys included.** Theorem 2 is stated for a group of prime order `q` (p. 27), and ICDH oracle calls are defined only on group elements. For Ed25519 the paper notes the cofactor 8 and the need to check or force subgroup membership (p. 75). It also says the receivers' public keys must be checked "to be an element of G", "once and for all" (p. 76). Theorem C calls the oracle on `(PK_j, V*, ·)` for corrupt `PK_j` (Game 5), so suite 3 needs `[ℓ]PK_j = O` at key registration. SCHEME §3.4 check 2 covers the points of a message, and C1 covers the range of `HG_eph`. SCHEME §2 "Setup" does not yet state the check for registered keys.

5. **Theorem C is static only, and it is composed with Theorem A through a hybrid.** For adaptive corruption, Theorem 5 (pp. 62 to 64) uses a different game with an `n²` loss and the signature-based restrictions of item 1. Nothing in it carries over to Theorem C, and PROOFS.md claims nothing adaptive. Appendix A.6 (pp. 97 to 99) warns that programming random oracles built from SHA-256 "could conceivably interfere with the reductions to collision resistance (CR), random preimage resistance (RPR), and zero preimage resistance (ZPR) of SHA-256". The paper's answer is that the outer simulator programs only "benignly" (for Fiat-Shamir simulation), while the full programming of the MEGa analysis serves only to show that the simulation is faithful. Line dealing has the same structure. Theorem C's full programming of `HG_eph` and `Hq_enc` stays inside the step from `Exp` to `Exp'`. Theorem A then runs in `Exp'`, where the only programming simulates the authorship and DLEQ proofs. This conditions the use of Theorem A on the suite 1 sighash (untagged double SHA-256) in the same way, and changes nothing in Theorem C.

Two smaller points, both inside section 4, check out. The ICDH oracle is defined with "one of the first two entries in the triple must be u" (p. 29), while the proofs call it on `(·, v*, ·)` (pp. 29, 63). Every call in Theorem C has `V*` as its second entry, so Theorem C matches the form the proofs use, which is how PROOFS.md §1.2 states the assumption. And the proof of Theorem 2 is itself a sketch ("The above is not really a complete proof", p. 31), with hops that are only "essentially equivalent" and no concrete bound. The bound `δ_C` in Theorem C is PROOFS.md's own derivation and gets no support from the paper beyond the game order.
