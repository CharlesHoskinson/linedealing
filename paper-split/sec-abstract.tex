\begin{abstract}
A Schnorr or ECDSA signature is one point on a line whose two coefficients the key holder knows. Line dealing lets the owner of an existing address deal that line to a committee in one message, so that the committee signs one spend later~\cite{LD}. Split-line dealing cuts the line at address birth. The committee holds one half, $\xf$, in Shamir form. Outside parties, the \emph{actors}, hold the rest as shards, and the address key is $x = \xf + \tw + \sum_a x_a$ with a secret tweak $\tw$. Nobody holds $x$. A charter fixes at birth a monotone access structure over the actors, built from AND, threshold and OR gates, and the committee is always ANDed with it. To spend, an initiator posts one fixed-size envelope, and each named actor makes one offline move: its shard evaluated at the challenge of every transaction of a fee grid that spends one pinned input. The committee selects one variant per fee generation from chain state and adds its half with one linear partial per node. The sum is an ordinary single-key BIP340, Ed25519 or ECDSA signature. No secret multiplication runs at signing time; ECDSA pays for its non-linearity once, in an interactive session at birth that prepares one-use slots.

The committee half needs only a challenge scalar, a binding factor and a nonce slot, so a node co-signs blind: before release it sees an address-free ticket, and in the mode with confidential VMs its operator sees ciphertext, sizes and timing. The outer dealing of an envelope deals one secret, and every key the committee derives from it, for the header, the variant bodies and the sealed contributions, is the output of a threshold PRF whose partials leave a node only after the board orders the envelope. Three custody profiles share one envelope and one ordering path: the owner's existing key, co-custody, and a regulated profile that adds a guardian branch and holds.

The algebra and the counting behind the design are machine-checked in Lean~4: the key split, the weighted reconstruction for every nested access formula, secrecy of the scalars of a coalition outside the formula, the leak of a group's half line when two leaf sets answer one ECDSA slot, slot precedence by board order, slot burns at a cold start, and, for the single-user and guardian charters, the independence of signing from the profile. The cryptographic claims are sketched or open, and the paper names the open obligation of each. A prototype measures the costs on one core: at nine nodes a request costs a node \estCpuTotalNine{}, so one core serves \estRateNine{} requests per second. Dealing and checking committee slots take three quarters of that work and the request path the rest.
\end{abstract}
