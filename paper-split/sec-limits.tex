\section{Limits and open problems}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{An admitted set with $t$ nodes holds the key.} An admitted signing set that colludes with $t$ committee nodes, in non-TEE mode or with $t$ broken enclaves, holds $x$. In non-TEE mode without slot anchors fewer nodes suffice. A coalition of $f < |C| < t$ nodes that forks the board gets $t$ partials on each of three tickets of one Schnorr slot once $3(t - |C|) \le n - |C|$: six nodes at $n = 9$, ten at $n = 17$. The three committee halves give $\xf$ (\cref{sec:slots}). With an admitted set on ECDSA two histories suffice, $2(t - |C|) \le n - |C|$, five nodes at $n = 9$ and seven at $n = 17$: two signatures on one slot share the nonce and give $x$. Covenants, vetoes and holds of P1 and P2 are therefore guarantees of an honest threshold, or of the TEE, and not of cryptography. What survives every corruption of the committee is C3, C4 and C17: no signature without an admitted set.

\paragraph{A silent actor.} A charter whose every admitted set needs one actor cannot spend while that actor is silent, and loses the coins if the actor disappears for good, unless the charter gives that actor an OR branch or a threshold group. Reissue uses such a branch; nothing else replaces it.

\paragraph{Released signatures.} A signature released to a host chain cannot be recalled. A matured Bitcoin exit leaf pays any destination and ends every covenant and hold for that coin.

\paragraph{ECDSA.} The birth session needs every session party online, at birth and at each refill, and rests on two functionalities whose instantiation at $t = n - f$ is open. It runs in the clear on the board, which links ECDSA spends to their accounts (\cref{sec:blindness}). A whole-committee restart without slot anchors burns the unused slots of every ECDSA address, which then refill. An account's ECDSA slot ids are public to the committee, so precedence on them starts only from an authorization in force, and the end of a request burns an ECDSA slot only if that request reserved it (\cref{sec:ecdsa-slots}). An envelope without an authorization in force therefore voids no ECDSA request and burns no ECDSA slot, and a P2 user cannot delay a seizure by naming the guardians' slots. Two requests on one ECDSA slot never both come into force, because the requests of one source spend one nullifier and the two sources of a P2 record open disjoint slot roots. In the design Quint model the rule keeps its invariants on every run, and the old rule, precedence by board order, breaks them; the proof that the rule keeps one nonce use and liveness is open (O38).

\paragraph{Proof coverage.} All claims are for static corruption. The algebra and the counting are proved in Lean, every cryptographic claim is sketched or open, and the secrecy theorems cover scalars only. The state machine is tested by bounded random simulation in Quint, not by exhaustive model checking, and no artifact checks liveness as a temporal property. The costs are measured on one benchmark core, not on node hardware, and the figures marked $^{\ast}$ in \cref{sec:eval} are estimates.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A signature line can be cut. The committee keeps one half, the same for every address, and never learns a shard; the actors keep the rest under an access structure the charter fixes, and a spend is one move by each named actor and one linear partial by each node. The cut keeps what made line dealing worth having, a limit on what a fully corrupt committee can sign, and extends it to every actor of the charter: the scalars of a coalition that the charter does not admit are consistent with every value of the dealt secret, which Lean proves for every well-formed nested formula. It adds what line dealing lacked: nobody holds the key, and the committee co-signs a ticket that names no address. The algebra, the leaf-set binding, the slot precedence and the cold-start burn are machine-checked; the reductions that turn them into unforgeability and sealing theorems are the work that remains, and each has its name. The state machine around them is a specification that an executable Lean oracle states and a Quint model written from the specification alone tests, and that model shows seven of its rules to be needed.
