\section{Split-line dealing}\label{sec:construction}

We first give the birth of an address, then the committee's nonce slots, the contributions on the Schnorr suites, the ECDSA slots, the envelope, the committee's processing, the authorization, the TEE mode and the exit paths. A \emph{half} is the nonce material of one signature: input $j$ of variant $v$ in fee generation $g$.

\subsection{Address birth}\label{sec:birth}

One actor, the \emph{birth coordinator}, collects the actors' points and posts the birth request. In the single-user form it is the holder's wallet.

\paragraph{Shards.} Each leaf receives its secret. An actor picks its shard uniformly at random. A threshold group runs a key generation among its members at degree $k_\gamma - 1$, each dealer with a Schnorr proof of knowledge of its constant term bound to the birth context; $X_\gamma$ is the sum of the dealers' constant terms, and the Feldman commitments give every member point $X_{\gamma,i}$. A nested gate repeats this inside the group, so a leaf holds a share of a share and its weight is the product along its path. The primary holder of an OR gate deals its shard to the other child (\cref{sec:or}). A holder who is offline at birth can still receive a shard: it registers a receive key $U = uG$ once, a creator posts $E_c = e_c G$ encrypted to it and sets $X_u = U + \Hq{st}(e_c U)\,G$, and only the holder computes $x_u = u + \Hq{st}(u E_c)$.

\paragraph{Tweak and proofs of possession.} The coordinator picks a seed, computes $\tw$ as in \cref{sec:keys} and sends the seed to every actor. Each top-level shard carries
\[
  \mathsf{pop}_a = \mathsf{Schnorr}_{x_a}\bigl(\Hq{pop}(\Xf \,\|\, X_a \,\|\, \mathsf{slot}_a \,\|\, \mathsf{charter\_id} \,\|\, \mathsf{chain\_id})\bigr),
\]
bound to the actor's point, its charter slot, the committee key and the charter. A shard created for an offline holder carries instead the registration proof of $U$ and the creator's proof of knowledge of $\delta$ with $X_u - U = \delta G$. Without the proofs the last actor to publish chooses $X_r = zG - \Xf - \sum_{a \neq r} X_a$. The tweak is computed after $X_r$, so the address key is $z + \tw$, and the actor knows it as soon as it holds the seed (\cref{prop:keysplit}(d) with the tweak term set to zero). With them, an extractor obtains every corrupt shard, and a coalition that knows $z$ then knows $\xf$ plus the honest shards, which breaks the discrete logarithm. Every summand carries its own proof, so the order of publication does not matter and no commit round runs. The tweak is a scalar and not a point: a point $T$ in its place would let an actor set $T = zG - \Xf - \sum_a X_a$.

\paragraph{Birth check and record.} The coordinator sends a birth envelope (\cref{sec:envelope}) with every shard point and its proof, every group's dealer proofs and commitments, $\tw$, the access structure and a commitment $\mathsf{cm}_{\mathsf{addr}} = \mathsf{Commit}(X \,\|\, \mathsf{chain\_id} \,\|\, \mathsf{root} \,\|\, \mathsf{charter\_id} \,\|\, \mathsf{profile} \,\|\, H(\Gamma); r)$. After ordering and opening, each node checks every proof, computes $X$ and the address, checks that $\mathsf{cm}_{\mathsf{addr}}$ opens, and returns a signature on $(\eid, \mathsf{cm}_{\mathsf{addr}})$ to the coordinator over the node channel, not the board. A quorum of these signatures is the \emph{birth certificate}, and the authorization ledger accepts a P1 or P2 address record only with one.

On Bitcoin the key path signs for the Taproot output key $Q = g_P X + t_{\mathsf{tap}} G$ with $g_P = \pm 1$ chosen so that $g_P X$ has even $y$. With $g_Q = \pm 1$ for the parity of $Q$ and $g = g_P g_Q$, the signing key is $d = g x_A + g_Q t_{\mathsf{tap}} + g\,\xf$. The actors' contributions carry $g x_A + g_Q t_{\mathsf{tap}}$, with the terms of $\tw$ and $t_{\mathsf{tap}}$ in the initiator's contribution only, and the committee half carries $g\,\xf$, with the sign folded into its challenge.

\subsection{Committee nonce slots}\label{sec:slots}

A Schnorr-suite \emph{nonce slot} $\ell$ is a pair of committee nonces $(d_\ell, e_\ell)$ with public points $D_\ell = d_\ell G$, $E_\ell = e_\ell G$ and Feldman commitments. Each slot is the sum of the random polynomials of $t$ dealers, dealt on the board with the encryption and public complaint of line dealing, so no coalition of $t-1$ nodes knows $d_\ell$ or $e_\ell$. A dealer posts many slots in one message with one ephemeral key and one encryption proof, and a node checks its shares with one random linear combination whose 128-bit coefficients it draws after it holds the message; on failure it checks each polynomial alone and names the bad one. The slots of one suite in one epoch form one \emph{committee pool}, indexed in dealing order. The pool is public: an initiator fetches unclaimed slot points from any node.

\paragraph{One slot per generation and input.} A request runs at most $\Gmax = 2$ fee generations, and each generation selects a higher variant than the one before. Variant $v$ can be selected in generation $g$ only if $g \le \min(v, \Gmax)$, so a request with $K$ variants of $L$ inputs has
\[
  \Nh = L \sum_{v=1}^{K} \min(v, \Gmax)
\]
halves, 30 at $K = 8$, $L = 2$. Its header names one slot $\ell(g, j)$ for each generation and input, $\Gmax L = 4$ slots at that size, and every half of generation $g$ and input $j$ binds to $\ell(g, j)$. The committee releases one partial per slot: the partial for the ticket of the variant that the anchor of generation $g$ selects. The halves of the other variants on that slot are never completed. The $K$ candidate tickets of a slot give no party anything it lacks without them, because any party can evaluate the binding hash and the challenge hash on as many candidates as it likes; the committee's partial is computed at release, for one ticket.

\paragraph{Precedence and reservation.} A request \emph{names} a slot when its header, opened after ordering, lists it. The first request in board order that names a committee slot has \emph{precedence} on it while it is live, and honest nodes vote for no later request that names it: once that request ends, the slot is burned and no request has precedence on it. A request with precedence \emph{reserves} the slot when $q$ nodes vote one header hash for it while its authorization is in force. A named committee slot is \emph{burned} when the request that had precedence on it ends, whether or not a partial was released, and it never returns to the pool. Precedence by board order means that a node which reads a header after ordering cannot copy its slots into a request of its own and void the first. On a committee slot the rule ``the earliest request whose authorization is in force wins'' lets a later request take the slot from a live earlier one (\cref{prop:precedence}). An ECDSA slot takes precedence from an authorization in force instead (\cref{sec:ecdsa-slots}).

\paragraph{Reservation window.} Each checkpoint on the authorization ledger records four fixed fields per committee pool: the \emph{floor} $h$, the highest index reserved or retired; the \emph{pending floor}, the lowest index that a live request holds, or $h + 1$ when none does; a Merkle root over the sorted list of \emph{pending} indices, the slots of every live request; and the length of that list. The list itself stays on the board. Indices are reserved in increasing order, so every pending index lies between the pending floor and $h$. A header names only indices in $(h, h+B]$ of the latest checkpoint, or pending indices of its own request, and an honest node votes only for such a header, checking the pending list against its root. A header that names any other index at or below $h$ gets no honest vote and ends void; one that names an index above $h + B$ waits for a checkpoint. The ledger refuses a checkpoint whose pending floor exceeds $h + 1$, so no index escapes both the window and the pending range. The window bounds what a whole-committee restart must burn (\cref{sec:tee}). Sizing $B$, the pool and the initiators' choice of indices against the measured request rate is open (O34).

\paragraph{Agreement on a slot's ticket.} Honest nodes agree on the one ticket of a slot because they read one board: one order of requests and one anchor per generation. Board safety holds for $|C| \le f$. A coalition with $f < |C| < t$ in non-TEE mode can certify conflicting board histories and show different honest nodes different requests on one slot. Three histories with distinct binding factors and challenges give three equations $\sigma_b s_f^{(b)} = d_\ell + \rho_b e_\ell + \sigma_b \ctil_b \xf$ in three unknowns and reveal $\xf$. Each history needs $t$ partials, and the corrupt nodes sign in all three, so the attack needs $3(t - |C|) \le n - |C|$: six nodes at $n = 9$, ten at $n = 17$. The slot rules therefore hold against $|C| \le f$ in non-TEE mode. A \emph{slot anchor} restores them against $|C| < t$: each node appends the binding $\ell \mapsto H(\tk)$, keyed by an unlinkable slot nullifier, to its log on the board, and releases on the slot only after a ledger checkpoint root covers the entry and no covered entry of any node binds the nullifier to another ticket. It adds no ledger transaction and about one checkpoint interval plus ledger finality to each generation. Its specification and proof are open (O32).

\subsection{Contributions on the Schnorr suites}\label{sec:contrib}

Every actor that may contribute to a Schnorr signature keeps a \emph{nonce pool}: $N_{\mathsf{pool}} = 64$ pairs $(d_{a,\nu}, e_{a,\nu})$ with points $(D_{a,\nu}, E_{a,\nu})$, posted on the board under a fresh pseudonym key per pool. The actor registers one Merkle root over a batch of future pool keys, its \emph{pool-key root}, in the shielded charter record, and the authorization proves that every pool key the request names lies under a root of its actor, so the ledger shows no pool key. An actor answers at most once per pair and erases the pair when it answers. A request consumes one pair per half of each named actor.

Fix half $(v, g, j)$ with committee slot $\ell = \ell(g, j)$, signing set $S$ with initiator $a_0$, and for each other actor $a \in S' = S \setminus \{a_0\}$ its assigned pair $\nu$. On BIP340:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The initiator picks a fresh $k_0$ and sets $R_0 = k_0 G$.
\item The salted message digest is $\pmh = \Hq{pm}(x(Q) \,\|\, m \,\|\, \mathsf{salt})$, with $m$ the BIP341 sighash and a salt per variant, generation and input.
\item The \emph{commitment list} is $\CL = R_0 \,\|\, (\mathsf{leaf}_a \,\|\, \mathsf{pool}_a \,\|\, \nu \,\|\, D_{a,\nu} \,\|\, E_{a,\nu})_{a \in S'} \,\|\, \ell \,\|\, D_\ell \,\|\, E_\ell$, with the actors in leaf-id order. A leaf id is the actor's charter slot, its gate path and its member index, so two members of one group have different ids. Each actor's binding factor is $\rho_a = \Hq{bind\text{-}act}(\mathsf{suite} \,\|\, \mathsf{epoch} \,\|\, \mathsf{leaf}_a \,\|\, \CL \,\|\, \pmh)$.
\item The \emph{aggregate actor nonce} is $R_A = R_0 + \sum_{a \in S'} (D_{a,\nu} + \rho_a E_{a,\nu})$, and the committee's binding factor is $\rho = \Hq{bind}(\mathsf{suite} \,\|\, \mathsf{epoch} \,\|\, \ell \,\|\, R_A \,\|\, \pmh)$.
\item The nonce point is $R = R_A + D_\ell + \rho E_\ell$, and $\sigma = -1$ if $R$ has odd $y$, else $\sigma = 1$.
\item The challenge is $c = H_{\mathsf{BIP0340/challenge}}(x(R) \,\|\, x(Q) \,\|\, m)$, and $\ctil = g\,c$.
\item The contributions are
\[
  s_0 = \sigma k_0 + \ctil(\lambda_0 x_0 + \tw) + c\,g_Q t_{\mathsf{tap}}, \qquad
  s_a = \sigma(d_{a,\nu} + \rho_a e_{a,\nu}) + \ctil\,\lambda_a x_a \quad (a \in S').
\]
\item The \emph{ticket} for the committee is $\tk = (\ell, R_A, \pmh, \ctil, x(R), \mathsf{fee\_rate}, \mathsf{not\_before})$.
\end{enumerate}
Node $i$ computes the partial $s_{f,i} = \sigma(d_{\ell,i} + \rho\,e_{\ell,i}) + \ctil\,x_{f,i}$, which anyone checks as $s_{f,i} G = \sigma(D_{\ell,i} + \rho E_{\ell,i}) + \ctil X_{f,i}$. The node recomputes $\rho$, $R$ and $\sigma$ from $R_A$ and the slot points, and it releases nothing for a ticket whose $x(R)$ differs from its own. Lagrange combination of $t$ partials gives $s_f$, and $s = \sum_{a \in S} s_a + s_f = \sigma k + c\,d$ with $k = k_0 + \sum_{a \in S'}(d_{a,\nu} + \rho_a e_{a,\nu}) + d_\ell + \rho e_\ell$, because $\sum_{a \in S} \lambda_a x_a + \tw = x_A$ for every $S \in \Gamma$. So $(x(R), s)$ verifies under $x(Q)$. The tweak terms $\ctil\,\tw + c\,g_Q t_{\mathsf{tap}}$ appear exactly once, in the initiator's contribution: without $c\,g_Q t_{\mathsf{tap}}$ the signature fails under $Q$, and a second copy of $\ctil\,\tw$ in another contribution makes the signature fail (\cref{prop:keysplit}).

On Ed25519 the slot, the commitment list, the binding factors and $R$ are the same, with $\pmh = \Hq{pm}(X \,\|\, M \,\|\, \mathsf{salt})$, no parity rule and $c = \mathrm{SHA512}(R \,\|\, X \,\|\, M) \bmod q_s$:
\[
  s_0 = k_0 + c(\lambda_0 x_0 + \tw), \quad s_a = d_{a,\nu} + \rho_a e_{a,\nu} + c\,\lambda_a x_a, \quad s_{f,i} = d_{\ell,i} + \rho\,e_{\ell,i} + c\,x_{f,i}.
\]
Every scalar is uniform modulo $q_s$, so clamping of seed-derived keys plays no role.

\paragraph{Binding.} The pool pairs of every actor other than the initiator are public before any message exists, as the committee's slots are, and an adversary that controls the initiator and other actors may open many requests that name one honest actor's pairs. Each $\rho_a$ hashes the whole list $\CL$ and $\pmh$, which fix $R_A$, hence $\rho$, $R$ and $c$. So no adversary chooses $c$ as a function of $E_{a,\nu}$ after seeing it. This is the two-nonce binding of FROST~\cite{FROST,RFC9591}, with every actor and the committee as signers. A single pre-published nonce per pair would be open to the ROS attack on concurrent sessions. The initiator uses one fresh nonce per half, because it computes its contribution after $\CL$ is fixed and reveals $R_0$ only with it. No actor nonce serves two halves, even two variants of one input on one committee slot: all contributions are sealed in advance and the committee holds them, and two of them on one nonce give the shard by subtraction.

\paragraph{The actor's rules.} Before it contributes, an actor reads every variant's transaction, recomputes every sighash, $X$, $Q$, the signs, $\pmh$, $\CL$, every binding factor, $R_A$, $R$, $\sigma$ and $c$ from the request and the public pool and slot points, and checks the ticket's $R_A$. It takes no weight, no nonce point and no challenge from the initiator. It checks that $S \in \Gamma$, that its weight follows from $S$, and that the pairs named for it are unused pairs of its own pool, one per half. It checks the transactions against its own policy and may refuse. It answers once per pair and never after the collection deadline.

\subsection{ECDSA slots}\label{sec:ecdsa-slots}

ECDSA is not linear in the nonce, so the split is made in advance, in one interactive session per address among the committee and every \emph{session party}: every actor that holds a top-level shard and every member of every group. The escrow holder of an OR gate is not a session party (\cref{sec:or}). Each session party $p$ enters with an additive part $x'_p$ of $x_A$: an actor's shard, or, for a member of group $\gamma$ with a fixed birth subset $T_0$ of $k_\gamma$ members, $\lambda_{p,T_0} x_{\gamma,p}$ if $p \in T_0$ and zero otherwise; party $1$ adds $\tw$. Each slot $\ell$ ends with a point $R_\ell = kG$ and additive halves of both line coefficients,
\[
  w_f + \sum_p w_p = k^{-1}, \qquad \mu_f + \sum_p \mu_p = k^{-1} x ,
\]
with the committee's $(w_f, \mu_f)$ in Shamir form at threshold $t$. The session uses two functionalities. $F_{\mathsf{presig}}$ gives the committee a threshold presignature for $\xf$: a point $R_f = k_f G$ and Shamir shares of $\varphi = k_f^{-1}$ and $\psi = k_f^{-1}\xf$, with Feldman commitments in base $R_f$ whose constant terms are $G$ and $\Xf$. $F_{\mathsf{mul}}$ turns two parties' scalars into an additive sharing of their product. The candidates are the presigning of Canetti et al.~\cite{CGGMP21} and the two-party emulation of 2PC-MPC~\cite{TwoPCMPC} for $F_{\mathsf{presig}}$, and oblivious-transfer or Paillier-based multiplication for $F_{\mathsf{mul}}$. Their instantiation at $t = n - f$ is open (O1, O2), and so is the session among many parties (O19).

Per slot, the committee runs $F_{\mathsf{presig}}$; each party in turn multiplies the nonce point by a fresh $k_p$, so $R_\ell = (\prod_p k_p) R_f$; the parties and nodes run $3n_A(n_A-1)/2$ party-to-party and $3n_A n$ party-to-node instances of $F_{\mathsf{mul}}$; and each party sends each node a correction, a Shamir share of its own half, that makes the node's shares $(w_{f,i}, \mu_{f,i})$ shares of $(w_f, \mu_f)$. No proof system checks the result. Each party publishes commitments in base $R_\ell$ to the polynomials of its halves and the products $x'_p \Phi$ with one batched equality-of-logarithms proof, and each node checks its shares against the identity $(a\varphi_i) R_\ell = \varphi_i R_f$, where $a = \prod_p k_p^{-1}$. The implied constant terms satisfy $W_f + \sum_p W_p = G$ and $M_f + \sum_p M_p = X$ in base $R_\ell$, so a party that corrupts the products is caught, and a node whose share fails posts a complaint that drops the slot. Each member of a group then deals its halves to the group at degree $k_\gamma - 1$, and member $i$ holds Shamir shares $(w_{\gamma,i}, \mu_{\gamma,i})$ of the group's half $(w_\gamma, \mu_\gamma)$.

\paragraph{Contribution.} For input $j$ of variant $v$ the initiator takes an unused slot with $r = x(R_\ell) \bmod q_s$ and sighash $h$. Each leaf of $S$ contributes
\[
  s_a = \lambda_a(h\,w_a + r\,\mu_a), \qquad\text{checked as } s_a R_\ell = \lambda_a(h W_a + r M_a),
\]
and node $i$ computes $s_{f,i} = h\,w_{f,i} + r\,\mu_{f,i}$. The sum is $s = h k^{-1} + r k^{-1} x = k^{-1}(h + r x)$, an ordinary ECDSA signature (\cref{prop:keysplit}). The ticket is $(\ell, h)$. A slot signs one message: two contributions with $h \ne h'$ on one slot give $(w_a, \mu_a)$, and with every other half $k$ and $x$. The slot sharing of \cref{sec:slots} therefore does not apply. A request keeps $KL$ ECDSA slots, one per variant and input, and unselected variants burn theirs.

\paragraph{One leaf set per slot.} The session binds each slot $\ell$ to one \emph{leaf set} $T_\ell$: one $k_\gamma$-subset of every threshold gate and one child of every OR gate on the path. The slot record carries $H(T_\ell)$, and a member of $T_\ell$ contributes on slot $\ell$ only to a request whose signing set has the leaf set $T_\ell$, and only once. Without the binding, two subsets $T \neq T'$ of one group could answer on one slot for two requests with sighashes $h \ne h'$, while each honest member answers only once. They give two evaluations
\[
  u = h\,w_\gamma + r\,\mu_\gamma, \qquad u' = h'\,w_\gamma + r\,\mu_\gamma
\]
of the group's half line, so $w_\gamma = (u - u')/(h - h')$, and $\mu_\gamma$ when $r \ne 0$ (\cref{prop:leafsets}). That leaks the group's half line on the slot, and only that. Recovering $x$ needs, in addition, the halves of every other session party on the slot and the committee's: $x = (\mu_\gamma + \mu_o)/(w_\gamma + w_o)$, where $(w_o, \mu_o)$ sums every other half. A fully corrupt committee has its own halves, so in $\mathsf{user} \wedge \mathcal{G}$ an honest user's halves still block the recovery of $x$, while the coalition gets the group's half line. The committee cannot stop two subsets when every node is corrupt, so the members enforce the binding. A slot reserved for the escrow branch of an OR gate is likewise never used by the primary holder. The birth session spreads its slots over the leaf sets the charter expects to use, and a refill session adds slots for one leaf set. A test that every member refuses a slot of another leaf set is open (O33).

\paragraph{Precedence on an ECDSA slot.} An ECDSA slot belongs to one address, and the committee knows its id. Under precedence by board order anyone could name an account's slot ids in envelopes that need no authorization, void with \msg{SLOT\_TAKEN} the requests that use those slots, and burn them when the envelopes end; a P2 user could burn the guardians' slots this way and stay offline, and the refill needs every session party online. A request therefore has precedence on an ECDSA slot only while its authorization is in force and only when the slot lies under the slot root that the authorization's source opens. Of the requests that meet both conditions and name the slot, the earliest in board order has precedence. A request without an authorization in force voids no other request on an ECDSA slot, and its end burns no ECDSA slot. An ECDSA slot burns when the request that reserved it ends. A slot that no request reserved holds no partial, because no node releases before the authorization is in force, so it needs no burn. A copy of a header takes no ECDSA slot: the copy needs an authorization in force for the same record, and the ledger accepts one authorization per nullifier. The rule has a cost: an authorized request can take a slot from an earlier namer without an authorization, which ends void with \msg{SLOT\_TAKEN} once its own authorization comes into force, and only the record's own sources can be in that position. A proof that the rule keeps C12 for ECDSA slots and gives C13 against such namers is open (O38).

\subsection{OR gates by escrow}\label{sec:or}

An OR gate $a \vee \gamma$ gives the second child a copy of the first child's secret. At birth the primary holder posts an escrow dealing to $\gamma$ in the open mode of line dealing with $\gamma$ as the committee: on the Schnorr suites $\Share_{k_\gamma}(x_a)$ with implied constant term $X_a$ and the tweak seed sealed to every member; on ECDSA the half lines $(w_a, \mu_a)$ of the reserved slots, in base $R_\ell$. The members acknowledge on the board, and a record whose $\Gamma$ has an OR gate is valid only with a quorum of their acknowledgements. In P2 the guardian group $\mathcal{G}$ holds the user's shard at $3$-of-$5$ in this way and contributes for a settled seizure from its own nonce pools. Guardians never hold $\xf$ and the committee never holds the escrow, so neither group can seize alone.

\subsection{The envelope}\label{sec:envelope}

Every request type, a birth, a spend, a slot refill, a contribution or a P0 line-dealing spend, travels in one frame of $16{,}384$ bytes, padded under the AEAD with random bytes. The public part names no chain, suite or variant count, so ordering is blind to the request type.

\paragraph{One secret.} The envelope deals one scalar, the \emph{data key} $\kappa_0$: one Feldman-committed $t$-of-$n$ Shamir sharing to the nodes' dealing keys, with the share encryption, the encryption proof $\pi$ and the public complaint of line dealing. Let $\dd$ be the digest of this outer dealing and $\Hc$ a hash to the curve. For a label $\mathsf{lab}$ the \emph{PRF point} is
\[
  Z_{\mathsf{lab}} = \kappa_0 \, \Hc(\dd \,\|\, \mathsf{lab}),
\]
and node $i$ computes its partial $Y_{i,\mathsf{lab}} = \kappa_{0,i}\,\Hc(\dd \,\|\, \mathsf{lab})$. Any $t$ partials combine to $Z_{\mathsf{lab}}$, and nobody reconstructs $\kappa_0$. The header key is $k_{\mathsf{hdr}} = \KDF(Z_{\mathsf{hdr}})$. For variant $v$ in generation $g$, $Z_{v,g}$ gives the body key $k_{v,g}$, the contribution secret $y_{v,g} = \Hq{q}(Z_{v,g} \,\|\, \texttt{ctb})$ and the contribution public key $P_{v,g} = y_{v,g} G$. A partial is checked only when the AEAD tag fails: the receiver then asks for Chaum-Pedersen proofs whose challenge hashes the whole statement, the partial $Y_{i,\mathsf{lab}}$ included, drops each bad partial and combines again. Because $\dd$ is inside $\Hc$, a partial opens keys of one outer dealing only, and the board orders no second envelope of an epoch with the same $\dd$. The envelope digest $\hd$ covers everything but $\pi$ and the authorization reference, and the envelope id $\eid = H(\hd \,\|\, \mathsf{auth\_ref})$ is the context of $\pi$, so a rewritten copy fails the public checks. An envelope with no ledger authorization, such as a birth, a refill or a contribution frame, carries 32 random bytes in place of the reference, so the field does not show the request type. The PRF keys are the only keys the committee derives. The capsule keys are random keys of the initiator, carried in the variant bodies, and a P0 envelope carries under $k_{\mathsf{hdr}}$ the sealed line dealings of line dealing itself, which the committee processes as line dealing does.

\paragraph{Contents.} The \emph{header}, under $k_{\mathsf{hdr}}$, holds the request type, $K$, $L$, the generation count and the slot of each generation and input, the ticket of every half, the actor slot table with a commitment to each actor's contribution token, the commitment opening that ties the authorization to the tickets, the keys $P_{v,g}$, and the selection rule; in TEE mode it also holds the intent and every weighted leaf point, so the enclave recomputes every sighash and checks every contribution. Each \emph{variant body} $(v, g)$, under $k_{v,g}$, holds the initiator's scalars for that body and the key of capsule $v$. Each \emph{capsule}, under its own random key, holds the unsigned transaction of variant $v$ with its prevouts, the initiator's nonce points, the salt seed, the initiator's term point and, for each actor, its pool id, its pair indices and its weighted leaf point. Each \emph{actor capsule} is an HPKE ciphertext to the actor's request key at a fixed position from the end of the frame. It holds $k_{\mathsf{hdr}}$, the actor's position, its token and the capsule keys, so the actor reads the whole request. It holds no $\kappa_0$, so the actor opens no variant body and never sees the initiator's scalars or another actor's contribution. Every actor capsule carries eight capsule keys, the unused ones zero, so it has one size, 385 bytes, for every $K$. A fixed \emph{layout block} $\mathsf{lay}$ of 64 bytes follows the outer dealing, encrypted under $k_{\mathsf{lay}} = \KDF(Z_{\mathsf{hdr}} \,\|\, \texttt{layout})$, and gives the lengths of the header, the 15 body regions and the 8 capsule regions, zero for each unused one. A node finds every region with one layout open and one header open, and the actor capsule carries $k_{\mathsf{lay}}$ beside $k_{\mathsf{hdr}}$. The public part, the layout block and the actor capsules have one size for every request, so ordering stays blind to $K$, $L$ and the generation count.

\paragraph{Sealed contributions.} An actor deals nothing. For each variant body it picks an ephemeral $e$ and posts
\[
  \bigl(E_{a,v,g} = eG,\ \mathsf{AEAD}(\KDF(e P_{v,g} \,\|\, E_{a,v,g} \,\|\, \dd \,\|\, \mathsf{position} \,\|\, v \,\|\, g),\ s_{a,v,g,1} \,\|\, \dots \,\|\, s_{a,v,g,L})\bigr),
\]
inside a \emph{contribution frame}: an ordinary envelope of its own type, with its own outer dealing, so that it has the common public shape. An actor that answers before the post has its contribution embedded in the header instead. The committee opens a contribution with $y_{v,g} E_{a,v,g}$ once it has derived $y_{v,g}$, which happens only for a released variant. An actor cannot check that $P_{v,g}$ is the PRF key of its label, because it does not hold $\kappa_0$; the combiner checks $y_{v,g} G = P_{v,g}$ at release and voids the request on a mismatch.

\paragraph{Order and open.} At the quorum certificate that orders the envelope, each node decrypts its share of $\kappa_0$, checks it against the commitments, and sends its header partial to every other node at once; a node whose share fails posts the public complaint and sends nothing. No partial leaves a node before that certificate. Any $t$ partials give $k_{\mathsf{hdr}}$. The node checks that the header is well formed, that this request has precedence on every slot it names, that all points are distinct, that the authorization is in force and commits to $\hd$ and the tickets, and in TEE mode that every recomputed sighash and challenge matches its ticket.

\paragraph{Collect.} Each named actor reads the request through its actor capsule, applies the actor's rules, and posts its contribution frame before the deadline $\mathsf{exp}_c$. The first well-formed frame per actor slot in board order is attached. If no admission certificate forms by $\mathsf{exp}_c$, every honest node voids the request, the reserved slots burn, and the initiator reissues with a new signing set that omits the silent actor, new slots, new pairs and a new authorization. A charter whose every admitted set needs one actor cannot reissue while that actor is silent; an OR branch or a threshold group avoids that.

\paragraph{Select, witness, release, combine.} For each generation, each node computes the median fee rate of the anchor and selects variant $j_g$ by the header's rule, then recomputes $\rho$, $R$ and $\sigma$ for the $L$ tickets of $(j_g, g)$ and compares $x(R)$. Before each release a node checks on the authorization ledger that the authorization is not revoked, at a finalized ledger height at least as new as the heights in the generation's anchor reports, each of which carries the reporting node's finalized ledger height beside its host height. In P2 the charter's hold authority reads $\rh$ and may revoke the authorization, and generation~$1$ is released only once the authorization has been final for $\Delta_{\mathsf{hold}}$ ledger blocks (launch value~$2$), which gives the hold authority its window. A revocation voids the request if nothing was released and stops every later generation otherwise. A TEE node then records the release at $w = n - 1 - f$ peers and binds each slot to its ticket (\cref{sec:tee}). The node sends its PRF partial for the body $(j_g, g)$ and its signature partials to the combiners: $f+1$ nodes chosen by $H(\eid \,\|\, g)$ in non-TEE mode, every enclave in TEE mode. A combiner derives $k_{j_g,g}$ and $y_{j_g,g}$, decrypts the body, opens the capsule and every sealed contribution, interpolates $s_f$ without checking it, and verifies the whole signature. Only if it fails does the combiner check partials against their commitments and contributions against their equations, drop a bad partial, or name the failing actor. A failing contribution ends the request, and the initiator reissues. Every honest node releases at most one variant per generation and computes a partial for at most one ticket per slot.

\paragraph{Finish.} At confirmation depth each node votes \msg{FINAL}. When a request ends, each node signs on the board its \emph{settlement leaf} $(\auth, \mathsf{outcome})$, and the next checkpoint carries one Merkle root over the leaves of every request that ended in the interval; the committee writes nothing to the ledger per request. The outcome is final, void with nothing released, or released: a request on which any partial was released never settles back to unspent, because its signed transaction may still confirm, so the record stays locked to the pinned input, and every later authorization must spend that input. The leaf and the intent fix the record's successor, and the next authorization on the successor proves in zero knowledge that the leaf lies under a landed root, so a spend after FINAL waits at most one checkpoint interval plus ledger finality. A reissue after a stall instead carries the VOID leaf with $q$ signatures in its own authorization and waits for no checkpoint. Honest nodes sign one outcome per authorization, and two quorums share an honest node, so no authorization settles two ways. Each node then erases the slot shares, its share of $\kappa_0$, every derived key and every opened scalar of the request.

\subsection{Authorization}\label{sec:auth}

The initiator submits an authorization to the ledger when it posts the envelope. It publishes
\[
  \auth = H(\ic \,\|\, \hd \,\|\, \tkc \,\|\, \nf \,\|\, \rh),
\]
with $\ic$ a hiding commitment to the intent, $\tkc$ a hiding commitment to the tickets padded to 32 entries, $\nf$ the nullifier of the address record's current state and $\rh$ a key-private encryption of the record's hold tag. The proof shows that the record exists and is unspent (or released, and the intent spends its pinned input); for a successor record it shows the settlement leaf of the request that produced it under a landed root. It also shows that the inputs belong to the record and the outputs respect the fee ceiling, that exactly one admitted source authorizes the intent with every check of that source, that $S \in \Gamma$ with the initiator in $S$, that every pool key the intent assigns lies under a pool-key root of its actor, that $\tkc$ opens to the intent's tickets, and on ECDSA that every slot lies in the record's slot root for that source. One circuit serves every suite, profile and grid size, so the record shows none of them. The ledger rejects a second authorization on one nullifier. No node votes admission and no node releases before the authorization is final on the ledger, because a signature released to a host chain cannot be recalled. The board orders and opens the envelope while the authorization finalizes. Encoding $\Gamma$ in the circuit is open (O22), and so are the settlement and pool-key paths in the circuit (O37).

\paragraph{The ticket proof.} In non-TEE mode the proof also shows, for each ticket,
\[
  \pmh = \Hq{pm}(x(Q) \,\|\, m \,\|\, \mathsf{salt}) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \ctil = g\,H_{\mathsf{BIP0340/challenge}}(x(R) \,\|\, x(Q) \,\|\, m),
\]
with $m$ the sighash of the committed intent, and the Ed25519 and ECDSA analogues. The ticket carries $x(R)$, which the node recomputes itself, so the proof holds hashes only and no curve arithmetic. Both relations are needed: without the $\pmh$ relation an initiator could hold $R$ fixed and search over messages, which reopens the attack that $\rho$ closes. The relations form one PLONK circuit at $2^{18}$ rows from the SHA-256, SHA-512 and Keccak gadgets of the ledger's proof library. Its row count and proving time are open (O4), and so is calling the gadgets from a ledger contract (O25).

\paragraph{Board-first.} A charter of profile P0, or of P1 whose predicates read no mutable ledger state, may let a node accept the authorization from the board. The initiator posts the authorization call beside the envelope with the ledger root it was proved against. Each node verifies the proof, checks that the root is a finalized ledger root no older than the charter's bound minus one checkpoint interval and the ledger's finality time, checks that no leaf of a landed board-first root holds $\nf$, and records $\nf$ as pending only after the proof verifies. The authorization is in force once the board orders the call as the first verified call for its $\nf$. The committee writes nothing to the ledger per call: the next checkpoint carries a Merkle root over the board-first calls of the interval, which a checkpoint signer signs only when no two of its leaves and no leaf of an earlier root share an $\nf$. The ledger takes no direct authorization for a board-first record, which changes only through a landed root, so a direct call cannot race the board. The request waits for no ledger finality. P2 and every charter with a cumulative limit or a veto cannot use it, because a hold cannot revoke what is not on the ledger. Its proof is open (O29).

\paragraph{Ledger writes.} The committee writes the ledger per checkpoint, per epoch and per boot, never per request. Share puts, slot bindings, release anchors and boot statements go to per-node logs on the board, settlements and board-first calls go to the board, and each checkpoint carries fixed-size roots over them. Only the users' own authorizations grow with the request rate. \Cref{tab:writes} counts the transactions.

\subsection{TEE mode}\label{sec:tee}

In TEE mode each node is a confidential VM whose trust base is the processor firmware and its signing chain, the paravisor measured into the launch report, the measured guest image, and the ledger. Operators, cloud attestation and key services are outside it. The state of a node lives in \emph{sessions}: records sealed under a key that the node derives from its own share and $w$ peers' partials of a seal-group threshold PRF, for one seal or one open, and never under a key that the disk alone holds. Committee slot shares sit in one sequential pool session per suite and epoch, and ECDSA slot shares in one account pool per address.

\paragraph{Witness.} Before any output that must happen once, a node records it at $w = n - 1 - f$ peers: a peer acknowledges a key with no entry or with the same digest and refuses a different one. A node binds each slot to the one ticket it signs with a witnessed entry and a sealed \emph{bound} state, and no partial on a slot leaves the node before that. Let $c$ operators collude and roll back or clone their nodes. Two acknowledgement sets of size $w$ among $n - 1$ peers meet in at least $2w - (n-1)$ peers, so a second digest needs $c - 1 \ge 2w - (n-1)$, that is $c \ge n - 2f$. The witness is safe for $c \le n - 1 - 2f$: four colluders at $n = 9$, six at $n = 17$. The count includes honest nodes that lost their state until they fetch it again from peers.

\paragraph{Cold start.} When fewer than $w + 1$ nodes are continuous with the latest ledger checkpoint, the restart is a whole-committee restart, decided from the ledger alone, because a rolled-back node cannot tell from its own disk. Each node then burns, in every committee pool, every index from the pending floor to $h + B$ of that checkpoint, a range that holds the window and every pending index, and every unused slot of every ECDSA account pool, and the next checkpoint raises the floor past the window. A slot reserved before the checkpoint can be bound after it, and a rolled-back disk shows it free, so the burn is needed (\cref{prop:coldstart}). A committee that anchors its slot bindings in its logs before release burns nothing: each slot with a covered binding becomes bound to that ticket. With fewer than $t$ intact nodes the recovery group reshares $\xf$ to fresh nodes; it reshares an ECDSA slot only when the checkpoint roots of anchored logs show that no node bound it, and otherwise every ECDSA address refills by a new session.

\subsection{Exit and recovery}\label{sec:exit}

An admitted signing set may always spend to any destination its charter admits. When no committee checkpoint reaches the ledger for $\Delta_{\mathsf{rec}}$ blocks, anyone may call a recovery transition, and the recovery group reshares $\xf$ to a successor committee whose charter mode admits only spends to the owner's standing exit record, signed by an admitted set. In P1 the exit has no policy checks. In P2 a live hold blocks it. On ECDSA the recovery group reshares only the slots that no anchored log shows bound, so without slot anchors the exit first runs a refill session with the successor, and that session needs every session party online. On Bitcoin a charter may add a tapscript exit leaf with a relative lock of $T_{\mathsf{exit}}$ blocks that a key of the actors spends alone; a matured leaf ends every covenant and hold for that coin. The reshare protocol at $t = n - f$ is open (O5), and so is the recovery group's protocol (O10).
