\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Line dealing.} Split-line dealing extends line dealing~\cite{LD}, which deals the line of an existing key to a committee as sealed points or as open lines, and which is profile P0 here. The outer dealing of every envelope, its share encryption, the public complaint, the anchor rule for fee generations and the one-move shape of the request come from it unchanged. What changes is what is dealt. Line dealing deals the owner's whole line, so a sealed point confines a fully corrupt committee and an open line gives a threshold of nodes the key. Here the committee half comes from the committee's own key generation and no owner deals it, every actor deals nothing but a point of its own shard line, and nobody holds the key.

\paragraph{Threshold Schnorr.} FROST introduced the two-nonce binding factor that defends threshold Schnorr against concurrent sessions, and RFC~9591 standardizes it with ciphersuites for Ed25519 and secp256k1~\cite{FROST,RFC9591}. The contributions of \cref{sec:contrib} use the same binding with every actor and the committee as signers. Here the actors are outside parties with their own pools, and they answer offline and sealed. One committee nonce is also shared by up to $K$ candidate tickets, of which the board selects one, and that sharing is why the unforgeability of the committee half (O6) and of pool actors (O20) needs a reduction of its own. Fully adaptive security for threshold Schnorr is treated by Crites et al.~\cite{Sparkle}; our claims are for static corruption.

\paragraph{Threshold ECDSA and presignatures.} The additive halves of an ECDSA slot are presignatures, and the security of ECDSA with presignatures and additive key derivation is the result of Groth and Shoup~\cite{GS21}, on which C3 and C17 rest for ECDSA. Their signing service fixes the request before it uses a presignature and re-randomizes it~\cite{GS22}. The public check of a linear ECDSA partial in base $R$ is in the signing phase of Gennaro and Goldfeder~\cite{GG20} and of Canetti et al.~\cite{CGGMP21}. Langford deals shares of the two line coefficients, $k^{-1}$ and $k^{-1}x$ in our notation, for each future signature to signers who answer with one linear partial~\cite{Lan95}. The presigning of Canetti et al.\ is a candidate for the functionality $F_{\mathsf{presig}}$ of \cref{sec:ecdsa-slots} among the committee. What is new in our session is that outside parties hold additive halves of the same slot and that a public identity in base $R_\ell$ checks the result with no proof system.

\paragraph{User-share custody.} The 2PC-MPC line keeps an owner share and lets the owner post one partial signature that a large network completes when a condition holds~\cite{TwoPCMPC,FMMSS25,IkaDocs}. It is the nearest published shape to the single-user form of P1. There the owner's message consumes a presignature that the network made first. One message authorizes one signature and no fee grid, and the network sees what it signs. Split-line dealing generalizes the owner to an access structure of actors, keeps a grid of conflicting variants of which the board selects one, and lets the committee sign a ticket without the address. Budgeted threshold signatures enforce a spending limit inside committee signing with an honest majority~\cite{CZC26}, which is a policy limit of the kind that the covenants of P1 are. Deployed threshold systems sign for standing keys of the system itself~\cite{ckBTC,THORChain,tBTC}, and the HOT protocol keeps the key with a committee~\cite{HOT}. One-message publicly verifiable dealings move a chosen key into a committee~\cite{cgVSS,Groth21}; our birth needs none, because no shard ever reaches the committee.

\paragraph{Position.} We know of no work that splits a dealt signature line between a threshold committee and outside shard holders over a nested access structure, keeps sealed confinement for every actor, and lets the committee co-sign blind.
