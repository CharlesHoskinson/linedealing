\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Line dealing.} Split-line dealing extends line dealing~\cite{LD}, which deals the line of an existing key to a committee as sealed points or as open lines, and which is profile P0 here. The outer dealing of every envelope, its share encryption, the public complaint, the anchor rule for fee generations and the one-move shape of the request come from it unchanged. What changes is what is dealt. Line dealing deals the owner's whole line, so a sealed point confines a fully corrupt committee and an open line gives a threshold of nodes the key. Here the committee half comes from the committee's own key generation and no owner deals it, every actor deals nothing but a point of its own shard line, and nobody holds the key.

\paragraph{Threshold Schnorr.} FROST introduced the two-nonce binding factor that defends threshold Schnorr against concurrent sessions, and RFC~9591 standardizes it with ciphersuites for Ed25519 and secp256k1~\cite{FROST,RFC9591}. The contributions of \cref{sec:contrib} use the same binding with every actor and the committee as signers. Here the actors are outside parties with their own pools, and they answer offline and sealed. One committee nonce is also shared by up to $K$ candidate tickets, of which the board selects one. That sharing needs no new oracle: the signing oracle of the committee half, one answer for each nonce pair after any number of hashed candidates, is the oracle of the partially non-interactive games of Bellare et al., whose Theorem~3 proves FROST2 secure at their level TS-SUF-2~\cite{BTA22}. The committee half differs from FROST2 in its Shamir-dealt nonce pair, its blind node, the factor and sign of BIP340 and the public actor part. The reduction with these four extensions is open (O6), and so is the reduction for pool actors (O20), whose binding hash takes the whole signing set as input, as the weighted assumption of the same paper requires. Crites et al.\ prove a three-round scheme adaptively secure up to half the threshold, and the revised version of that paper states that the result for the full threshold cannot hold without a further hardness assumption~\cite{Sparkle}. Chen proves full adaptive security for a FROST variant that masks nonces and responses, and the masks leave no partial signature that a third party can check~\cite{Chen25}. Each partial of a node here is checked against public share points, and our claims are for static corruption.

\paragraph{Threshold ECDSA and presignatures.} The additive halves of an ECDSA slot are presignatures, and the security of ECDSA with presignatures and additive key derivation is the result of Groth and Shoup~\cite{GS21}, on which C3 and C17 rest for ECDSA. Their signing service fixes the request before it uses a presignature and re-randomizes it~\cite{GS22}. The public check of a linear ECDSA partial in base $R$ is in the signing phase of Gennaro and Goldfeder~\cite{GG20} and of Canetti et al.~\cite{CGGMP21}. Langford deals shares of the two line coefficients, $k^{-1}$ and $k^{-1}x$ in our notation, for each future signature to signers who answer with one linear partial~\cite{Lan95}. Canetti et al.\ prove presigning for $n$-of-$n$ signers, with presignatures fixed to the signing set, and Doerner et al.\ and Nof and Parker prove signing for arbitrary thresholds with abort~\cite{CGGMP21,DKLs23,TroutPP}. None outputs threshold shares of the two coefficients with commitments in base $R$, which is what the functionality $F_{\mathsf{presig}}$ of \cref{sec:ecdsa-slots} asks of the committee (O1). What is new in our session is that outside parties hold additive halves of the same slot and that a public identity in base $R_\ell$ checks the result with no proof system.

\paragraph{User-share custody.} The 2PC-MPC line keeps an owner share and lets the owner post one partial signature that a large network completes when a condition holds~\cite{TwoPCMPC,FMMSS25,IkaDocs}. It is the nearest published shape to the single-user form of P1. One client message authorizes one signature and no fee grid, honest validators enforce the release condition, and the network sees what it signs. Spark splits each key between a threshold committee of operators and a user share that a threshold group of sub-users can hold, and it adds a second key decomposition for a delegate under a grant that the operators enforce~\cite{Spark}. Its operators see the transaction, and all operators together with a previous owner can spend twice. Iceberg nests a threshold group as one signer of MuSig2, with a proof~\cite{Iceberg}. Split-line dealing generalizes the owner to a nested access structure of actors who each answer offline, keeps a grid of conflicting variants of which the board selects one, and lets the committee sign a ticket without the address. Budgeted threshold signatures enforce a spending limit inside committee signing with an honest majority~\cite{CZC26}, which is a policy limit of the kind that the covenants of P1 are. Deployed threshold systems sign for standing keys of the system itself~\cite{ckBTC,THORChain,tBTC}, and the HOT protocol keeps the key with a committee~\cite{HOT}. One-message publicly verifiable dealings move a chosen key into a committee~\cite{cgVSS,Groth21}; our birth needs none, because no shard ever reaches the committee.

\paragraph{Blind co-signing.} In Larch a log service holds one share of every key of a user and co-signs ECDSA on a digest after it checks a zero-knowledge proof that the digest hashes a record that it logs in encrypted form. The log learns neither the public key nor the message~\cite{Larch}. The proof covers two parties, one of them corrupt. Statechain servers and co-signing services sign blinded MuSig2 challenges, and they stop an unauthorized signature by counting signatures or by a zero-knowledge proof of a spending policy~\cite{MercuryLayer,BlindVault,Sigbash}. None of these has a security proof, and the two that use zero-knowledge proofs claim no security under concurrent sessions. Fuchsbauer and Wolf prove a blind Schnorr signer secure under concurrent sessions when the requester commits before the signer's nonce and proves the blinded challenge well formed~\cite{FW24}. Our ticket is not blinded. A node sees the true challenge, which the authorization proof or, in the mode with confidential VMs, the enclave's own computation ties to an admitted transaction, and the address and the message stay hidden from a node that cannot enumerate them (\cref{sec:blindness}). What split-line dealing adds to these designs is a threshold committee under the blind half, nonces dealt before the request, and actors who answer offline.

\paragraph{Position.} Each part of the construction has a precedent taken alone. A threshold committee co-signs with a user-side key or group in Spark and in the 2PC-MPC line, and a threshold group signs as one MuSig2 key in Iceberg. A co-signer signs for a key and a message it cannot see in Larch, under a zero-knowledge proof of the digest, and in the blinded MuSig2 co-signers. A client message that a network completes later is the future signing of Ika. We know of no work that splits a dealt signature line between a threshold committee and outside shard holders over a nested access structure, keeps sealed confinement for every actor, and lets the committee co-sign blind.
